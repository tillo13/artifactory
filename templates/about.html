About Artifactory

A tiny public experiment in what happens if you publish a schema, let users design their own React views in Claude against it, and host their submissions multi-tenant — all over the same shared data.

One backend. Many AI-designed frontends.

Classic enterprise pattern: one team owns a canonical data model (ServiceNow CMDB, Salesforce objects, a house-build task list) and many other teams want their own custom views. Traditionally that means a frontend team per view, or a rigid low-code tool where everyone clicks together the same component library.

Artifactory tries a third path: publish the schema as a contract, let each person vibe-code their own frontend in Claude against it, and rehost the submissions on your domain where they can actually talk to your API.

Why this demo exists

Claude Artifacts are a fantastic design surface — you can vibe-code a working React app in minutes. But the artifact sandbox is airtight:

  • The CSP locks connect-src to a small CDN allowlist — your artifact can't reach your own backend.
  • window.storage caps at 20 MB per artifact, is Anthropic-hosted, and resets per-artifact-version.
  • Public artifact URLs are Cloudflare-Turnstile-gated — server-side fetching is blocked without a user session.
  • There's no "deploy this artifact for my friends to use" button.

So if you want multiple people to use a Claude-designed UI against your shared data, you have to flip the script: host the backend yourself (where CSP rules don't apply), publish a schema contract everyone rides on, let users design frontends in Claude, and rehost the submissions on your domain where their fetch() calls to /api/v1/* just work.

How it works

  1. 1 You pick a role from the 45-role dropdown. We mint you a session-backed user and pre-load a role-specific prompt for Claude.
  2. 2 You hit Open in Claude with prompt — the preamble goes straight into a fresh claude.ai chat. The preamble tells Claude your schema, your role, and the runtime's constraints.
  3. 3 Claude builds you a React artifact that obeys the contract. You iterate until it looks right.
  4. 4 You copy Claude's artifact JSX and paste it at /submit.
  5. 5 Your component mounts at /u/<your-id> in a React 18 + Babel Standalone shell with window.CURRENT_USER pre-injected and fetch() pointed at the shared backend. Share the link. Everyone sees your view, everyone sees the same data.

Stack

  • Backend: Flask, single main.py
  • DB: Postgres (Cloud SQL) in prod, SQLite locally
  • Render: React 18 + Babel Standalone — no build step
  • Styling: Tailwind via CDN
  • Hosting: Google App Engine F1, max_instances: 1
  • Auth: 30-day cookie session (no OAuth)
  • Rate limits: in-memory, per-IP, per-gunicorn-worker
  • Tests: 14 integration tests, run with python -m tests.full_test
